    How this calculator works

    Every number on the Home screen traces back to this chain of formulas. Follow it top to bottom — each step feeds the next.

    Step 1 · Inputs

    Today's expenses, in three streams

    General living expenses, healthcare (kept separate — it inflates faster), and any one-time cost like a future home purchase.

    ↓
    Step 2 · Inflate to retirement date

    Grow each stream forward by its own inflation rate

    Every rupee you'd spend today is worth more by the time you retire. Each stream compounds separately since healthcare typically inflates faster than general costs.

    FutureExpense = TodayExpense × (1 + inflation) ^ years to retirement
    ↓
    Step 3 · Size the required corpus

    Present-value a growing expense stream across your retirement years

    This is the core of the model: how much money, sitting invested on day one of retirement, is needed to fund an expense that itself keeps growing with inflation, for n years, while the remaining balance earns your post-retirement return.

    PV = P₀ × (1 − ((1+g)/(1+r))ⁿ) / (1 − (1+g)/(1+r))

    P₀ = first year's expense at retirement · g = inflation · r = post-retirement return · n = retirement years. Applied separately to the general and healthcare streams, then summed — and applied again (and subtracted) to any passive income you'll receive, since that income offsets what the corpus needs to cover.

    ↓
    Step 4 · Add one-time items

    Home purchase & post-60 knowledge fund

    A future home purchase (if you don't already own) and a ring-fenced upskilling fund are inflated to the relevant date and added as lump sums on top of the annuity above — they're one-off, not recurring, so they don't belong in the growing-annuity formula.

    ↓
    = Required Corpus

    The number you need on day one of retirement

    ↓
    Step 5 · Meanwhile — projected corpus

    What your current savings and future contributions grow into

    Your existing bank balance, stocks, bonds and other holdings compound forward at your pre-retirement return; your ongoing monthly savings compound as a growing monthly annuity alongside it.

    Projected = CurrentHoldings × (1+r)^years + FV(monthly savings annuity)
    ↓
    Compare

    Required Corpus vs Projected Corpus

    The difference is your surplus (you're on track, and the calculator shows what you could sustainably spend) or shortfall (it shows the extra monthly saving needed to close the gap).

    ↓
    Step 6 · Split into drawdown buckets

    The bucket-drawdown strategy

    Rather than holding the whole corpus in one place, it's split by when you'll need it — each bucket in a progressively higher-return, higher-volatility asset class, refilled from the next bucket every year or two.

    Bucket 1

    Years 1–3 of expenses, in liquid funds / short-term debt.

    Bucket 2

    Years 4–7, in medium-duration debt / hybrid funds.

    Bucket 3

    Years 8+, the remaining corpus, in equity for growth.

    ↓
    Alongside all of this · Accumulation glide path

    How you should be invested while still saving

    A separate, simpler rule of thumb for the years before retirement: equity allocation tapers by age band (roughly 70% equity at 40–50, 55% at 50–60, 35% at 60+), so you're carrying less market risk as retirement approaches.
